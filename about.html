The artist

Who was Sanzo Wada?

Sanzo Wada (1883–1967) was a Japanese painter, teacher and costume designer. He won the Academy Award for Best Costume Design for the film Gate of Hell, and in 1927 he founded the Japan Standard Color Association, the forerunner of today’s Japan Color Research Institute.

His six small volumes of color combinations, Haishoku Soukan, appeared in 1933–34. They are still used by designers and artists as a source of tested, harmonious palettes.

The data

About the colors

Every color and combination number was checked against the book’s printed color index. Screen colors are converted from the CMYK recipes with a generic print profile, so they are close to the printed swatches but not identical. Use the CMYK values for print work.

Questions

Frequently asked

What is A Dictionary of Color Combinations?

A book of 348 color combinations drawn from Sanzo Wada’s six-volume Haishoku Soukan of 1933–34, published in print by Seigensha.

How many combinations and colors are there?

348 combinations built from 159 colors: 120 use two colors, 120 use three and 108 use four.

Are the HEX codes exact?

They are close but not exact. The book gives each color as a CMYK print recipe, and the screen values here are converted from it with a standard print profile. Use the CMYK values for print work.

Is this the official book?

No. This is a free, unofficial companion and is not affiliated with Seigensha or with Sanzo Wada’s estate. The printed book is available from Seigensha.
